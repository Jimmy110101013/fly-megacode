Memory. Every Kenyon-cell axon is drawn, lobe by lobe, only as bright as what dopamine has left of its KC→MBON synapses there; the cells on the decision just taken light up on top. The fly is never told the right answer, and nobody keeps count for it. Its input is what a bystander could read off the patient: rhythm, ETCO₂, whether a line and a tube are in. Shocks delivered, time since epinephrine, which cycle this is: those are records of past actions, and they are held in its own central complex, 2,875 neurons and 171,030 recurrent connections wired and signed as in the connectome and driven by the real MBON→CX pathway. No weight in that circuit is trained. The only plastic synapses sit where the fly actually has plasticity, KC→MBON in the mushroom body. Each decision, it scores ten candidate actions, commits to one, and only then learns whether it was right: one bit, delivered as dopamine. PAM if correct, PPL1 if not.

Real, from the connectome: every neuron, every synapse, every sign, and the compartment wiring of dopamine onto the output neurons. Modelled: the sensory interface (a clinical state encoded as one-hot channels, since a fly has no eyes for an ECG), the ten-action menu, rate-based dynamics instead of spikes, and a handful of tuned parameters, sparsity foremost. Read it accordingly: this tests what the fly's own learning rule can do inside the fly's own wiring. It does not claim a fly could run a code.

Clinical correctness never passes through the fly. A state machine written from the AHA 2025 adult cardiac arrest algorithm does the grading, and every wrong answer shows the expected one above. Wiring is from FlyWire FAFB v783 (Dorkenwald et al.); annotations and traced skeletons from Schlegel et al., Nature 2024, all CC BY 4.0. Every line in the atlas is the traced morphology of a real neuron, dim at rest and lit when it fires. Both mushroom bodies are in the model, so both light up. Adenosine is left out on purpose: it belongs to the tachycardia algorithm, and this patient has bigger problems.

This is a neuroscience experiment, not a clinical tool. It is not for patient care, not ACLS training or certification material, and not reviewed or endorsed by the American Heart Association. The clinical authority is the AHA's published guidelines, not a simulated insect.
